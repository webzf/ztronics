The **MPU6050** is a popular 6-axis motion sensor that combines a **3-axis accelerometer** and a **3-axis gyroscope** in a single MEMS device. It communicates with microcontrollers such as Arduino, ESP32 and Raspberry Pi through the **I2C interface**, making it easy to integrate into motion-sensing and robotics projects.

The sensor can measure acceleration along the X, Y and Z axes while simultaneously measuring rotational motion around the same three axes. This makes the MPU6050 useful for detecting orientation, movement, rotation and changes in motion.

The MPU6050 also includes an integrated temperature sensor and Digital Motion Processor (DMP), providing additional capabilities for motion-processing applications.

> **Note:** The original MPU6050 is now listed by TDK as an **obsolete product**. It remains widely used in existing Arduino, ESP32 and maker projects, but newer designs should consider currently available alternatives.

---

## Key Features

* 3-axis accelerometer
* 3-axis gyroscope
* 6-axis inertial measurement unit (IMU)
* Configurable accelerometer range from ±2g to ±16g
* Configurable gyroscope range from ±250 to ±2000 °/s
* I2C digital communication interface
* Integrated temperature sensor
* Integrated Digital Motion Processor (DMP)
* Programmable interrupt system
* Low-power operating modes
* Suitable for Arduino and ESP32 projects

The accelerometer provides measurements of linear acceleration, while the gyroscope measures angular velocity around the X, Y and Z axes.

---

## Accelerometer

The MPU6050 includes a 3-axis accelerometer capable of measuring acceleration along three perpendicular axes.

Available full-scale ranges are:

* ±2g
* ±4g
* ±8g
* ±16g

The lower ±2g range provides higher sensitivity for applications involving relatively small movements, while higher ranges are useful for applications involving stronger acceleration or vibration.

---

## Gyroscope

The integrated 3-axis gyroscope measures angular velocity around the three axes.

Configurable full-scale ranges include:

* ±250 °/s
* ±500 °/s
* ±1000 °/s
* ±2000 °/s

This makes the MPU6050 suitable for applications requiring detection of rotation, orientation changes and angular movement.

---

## I2C Communication

The MPU6050 communicates digitally using the **I2C interface**, requiring only two communication lines:

* SDA — Serial Data
* SCL — Serial Clock

This makes it particularly convenient for microcontroller projects where multiple sensors and peripherals share the same I2C bus.

The MPU6050 can also be used alongside other I2C devices such as OLED displays and environmental sensors.

For example, an ESP32 can communicate with both an **MPU6050** and an **SSD1306 OLED display** using the same I2C bus.

## MPU6050 with ESP32

The MPU6050 works particularly well with the ESP32 because both devices support I2C communication.

A typical setup can combine the MPU6050 with an **[ESP32 DevKit V1](/products/esp32-devkit/)** to create motion-controlled projects, wireless sensors and interactive devices.

The ESP32 can process the MPU6050 measurements locally and use its built-in Wi-Fi or Bluetooth connectivity to transmit the resulting data to another device or service.

The MPU6050 and OLED display can share the same I2C bus, making the hardware connection simple.

## Digital Motion Processor

One of the notable features of the MPU6050 is its integrated **Digital Motion Processor (DMP)**.

The DMP can perform motion-processing tasks inside the sensor, reducing the amount of processing that needs to be performed by the host microcontroller.

This capability has made the MPU6050 popular in applications involving orientation and motion tracking.

---

## Power Requirements

The MPU6050 IC operates from a supply voltage in the range of approximately **2.375V to 3.46V**.

This is important when connecting the sensor directly to a microcontroller.

Many commercially available **MPU6050 breakout boards** include additional circuitry that allows them to be conveniently used with common development boards. However, the electrical characteristics of the individual breakout board should always be checked before connecting it to a specific microcontroller.
